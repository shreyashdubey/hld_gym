# The demo deck (Shipyard demo day, Sat 5 Sep, 16:00)

One file: `deck/demo/index.html`, hosted at **https://hld-gym.vercel.app/demo/**. Ten slides. Any PC, F11 (or `f`) for fullscreen, no login. Slides 5, 7, 9 play themselves when you land on them.

Keys: `→` / Space next slide · `←` back · `1`–`9` jump · End = slide 10 · `r` replay · `n` or `.` skip to the next phase of a playing experience (or click a phase name in the orange strip, or the `next ›` button) · `t` paper/dark · `f` fullscreen.

Names Rohit and Ananya are placeholders until you give me the real ones.

Four minutes: 20 · 20 · 15 · 15 · 60 · 15 · 30 · 15 · 30 · 15. Person A's experience runs to the end; leave B at RESET and C at "catches you".

## 1 · Hook

> Machines got good at thinking, so we stopped. If the answer is always on screen, what happens to understanding? I'm building the opposite of an answer machine: a book compiled for one person, from how they learn. Extract the person. Compile the book. The book fights back.

## 2 · The method

> By hand. I sit with someone and find out five things: what they know, how they think, how they learn fast, how the knowledge has to come out, and what makes it stick. The rest, the compile, the reels, the lock, the grader, is automated.

## 3 · What is built

> The first person was me. One screen, one idea. Reels. A checkpoint every chapter. And the lock: the diagram disappears, you rebuild it. Live, free, no signup.

## 4 · Shreyash

> Me. I want the story first, I read diagrams not paragraphs, and I forget both unless I redraw them. So every chapter starts with who got hurt, draws itself, then disappears.

## 5 · Shreyash's experience (plays itself)

> You tapped Pay, the signal died. Why weren't you charged twice? Watch: the story of the people it hurt, the mechanism drawing itself, the lock, me rebuilding it, the grade, and the question I'd miss.

## 6 · Rohit

> Rohit tests software by hand and wants to build for space. History first, learns by breaking it, believes his own test. So his book opens with a failed mission, runs on a board he can crash, and makes him write the test before the code.

## 7 · Rohit's experience (leave at RESET)

> A cosmic ray flips one bit, 400 km up. Apollo rebooted mid-descent and kept flying. Eight bits carry four bits of proof; one flip gets fixed. Two flips: the core hangs, the watchdog counts down, reset, mission continues.

## 8 · Ananya

> Ananya builds front-ends and wants exoskeletons. She thinks in state machines, needs to see it move, derives it then trusts it. So her book is a state machine with a spring in it, and the math arrives after the motion.

## 9 · Ananya's experience (leave after "catches you")

> You step off a curb; your knee catches you before you decide to. Gait as a state machine. Torque is a spring minus a damper. Too little damping throws you; enough catches you in fifty-eight milliseconds.

## 10 · Close

> Talk to a person, write their chapters, compile, next person. Three people, three books. Built for the next ten years, when the answer is always on screen and understanding isn't.

## If asked

- **Learning styles?** No, that doesn't replicate. What you don't know, how you think, and what the exam asks of you.
- **Automated?** The compile, the diagrams, the reels, the lock, the grader, the voice interviewer. The conversations and the verification are by hand.
- **Are B and C real?** Real people, real profiles, first chapters. Slides 7 and 9 show the design of their books; the system-design book is the fully built one.
