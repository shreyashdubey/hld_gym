LORIA, Nancy, 2005

They set out to save operational transformation.

Abdessamad Imine, Pascal Molli, Gérald Oster and Michaël Rusinowitch had a cost problem. A transformation function rewrites one editor's operation against another's, and proving one satisfies the second correctness condition on a string means "123 different cases to explore", redone whenever the function changes. So in 2003 they wrote the published functions in first-order logic and pointed SPIKE, an automated theorem prover, at them.

By December 2005 the report is flat: "we invalidate all previous propositions of transformation functions for string of characters by giving a counter-example for each".

Including their own. Their 2003 functions had passed because a precondition in the specification hid the case that breaks them. Rerun without it, SPIKE printed three sites editing "bcd", ending on "bxxd" and "bxd". A prover "proves what is specified. It does not prevent specification errors."

Every counterexample was one tie: "all the counter-examples are only instances of this tie". Two inserts either side of a character, deleted concurrently, nothing left to order them. WOOT removes nothing: every character gets an identifier that is never reused, and nothing is transformed. The 2011 survey files it as "a CRDT for concurrent editing". The OT branch copied it. Tombstone Transformation Functions are "a transposition of the WOOT approach ... in the OT framework".

The receipt, in their own paper: "the space overhead of tombstones grows indefinitely."

Google Docs deletes characters for real and converges. Write down what it has that these three sites did not.
