Tokyo, 2020

Fixed by mid-morning. Shut until the close.

At 7:04 a.m. JST on October 1, arrowhead, the Tokyo Stock Exchange's cash equity engine, could not read Device 1 of its shared disk. A memory module had killed the controller. Device 2 had 30 seconds to take over. At arrowhead's setting, automatic switching "does not function for the case of a failure resulting from a memory module failure." Fujitsu's manual said otherwise.

At 8:36 a.m. TSE cancelled the open for every listed issue. Halting runs through that same disk, so at 8:54 they cut the network. Manual switchover finished at 9:26 a.m.

They did not restart. Orders taken before 8:54 "had been matched and execution notifications had accumulated within arrowhead", unsent. Reconnect, and fills arrive from a session that never opened. Reboot, and members resend orders nobody told them were dead. Firms that could resend held about 38% of trading value, against the contingency plan's 50%. At 11:45 a.m. TSE president Koichiro Miyahara stopped the day: a full session of a $6 trillion market.

TSE later named what kept it shut "an absence of rules and procedures for trading resumptions": from April 2021, a reboot cancels every order the exchange holds and members resend. An exchange doesn't sell uptime. It sells one agreed order of events, and nobody could say what a restart does to an order a member still believes is live.

Your engine returns in ten seconds, book intact. Why might you still not be allowed to open? Write it first.
