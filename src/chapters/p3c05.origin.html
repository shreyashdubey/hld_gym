Sina Weibo, 2017

Weibo sorts its traffic into two kinds. One is on the calendar: Spring Festival, the same night every year, budget it months ahead. The other is one person going public about their love life. Fu Wen, who led Weibo's hybrid-cloud project, filed those in a category of their own — abrupt, and nobody warns you.

So Weibo stopped forecasting and started buying. With Alibaba Cloud it built DCP, a container platform aimed at one number: 1,000 nodes in ten minutes. At the 2016 Spring Festival it created more than 1,000 ECS instances and pushed 60% of core traffic through them.

Then noon, Sunday 8 October 2017. Lu Han posted one sentence, translated: "Hi everyone, I want to introduce my girlfriend @GuanXiaoTong to you." The platform was inaccessible for two hours. Server function was fully restored at around 4pm, a Weibo programmer said.

The fleet was elastic and it did not save them. A 2012 study of 80.8 million Weibo profiles and 7.2 billion follow relations found Weibo's following preference more concentrated and hierarchical than Twitter's — everyone points up. One tap at the top is not a bigger ordinary post, it is a different quantity of work. Ten minutes to a thousand machines makes that work arrive faster. It does not make it smaller.

Forty-five minutes, a whiteboard, and this exact problem: a hundred million timelines from one tap. Where would you refuse to do the work at all? Write it before you read on.
