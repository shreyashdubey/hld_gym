Tōhoku and Kantō, 2011

A rate limiter with no counter, at national scale.

The earthquake hit on 11 March 2011 and everyone in Japan reached for a phone. DoCoMo's network took 50 to 60 times the normal volume of traffic.

Nobody could count that. No carrier consults one shared counter per call attempt; at that volume the counter is the outage. So DoCoMo did not count callers. It picked one percentage and applied it region-wide — maximum capacity restrictions on voice calls of 80 percent, 90 percent in certain areas, across Tōhoku and Kantō. Every attempt was judged against that number and nothing else.

Packet was spared — a temporary 30 percent cap in Miyagi and nothing after, and nationally "either not restricted or restricted at a lower rate than voice traffic". The Disaster Message Board took about 3.79 million posts by 28 March. DoCoMo estimated roughly 6.0 billion yen of damage in the year to 31 March 2011. Japan's communications ministry reported in December 2011: more switch capacity, emergency priority calls kept viable, and voice messages carried over the packet network, which DoCoMo shipped in March 2012.

Not one of those is a better counter. A percentage is blind. A throttle discarding eight attempts in ten cannot tell a safety check from a redial, and nothing could teach it the difference in the moment.

Your limiter has to shed 80% of traffic in the next second. Write down what it would need to know to shed the right 80%.
