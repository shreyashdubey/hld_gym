HealthCare.gov, 2013

A deadline nobody could move, and a number nobody had computed.

The law required health insurance marketplaces by January 1, 2014. Federal enrollment opened 1 October. That date was not moving.

Two pre-launch assessments flagged capacity planning. A later audit: "Capacity requirements for hardware for the FFM system were not developed." Nobody had turned expected people into machines.

On 26 September, CMS technical officials tested at CGI Federal. The site could carry "far fewer concurrent (simultaneous) users than planned". They drove to the hosting contractor and asked for double. A manager, later: "We were pulling gear from all over the world, renting planes to get hardware here that was intended for other clients."

They doubled the wrong number. Every visitor had to enter through one account gate, the EIDM. The contractor who built it "did not know that all visitors to the website would have to enter through the EIDM system and, therefore, underestimated the capacity needed." It believed a shelved "anonymous shopper" tool would let people browse without an account.

CMS launched "without the required verification that it met performance requirements." Day one brought 250,000 concurrent users, by the U.S. CTO's count five times the number anticipated. Outages started within two hours. Six people finished an application. By March 2014, CMS had committed $840 million.

Hardware can be doubled in seventy-two hours. The arithmetic that would have said what to double was never done at all.

Which number should they have computed, and what would it have changed?
