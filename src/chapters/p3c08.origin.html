IRCTC and CRIS, 2016

Indian Railways sells its Tatkal quota one day ahead: AC at 10:00, non-AC at 11:00. In August 2016 a developer published a script that reads the Date header off IRCTC's login page, adds back half the round trip, and sets your clock to the server's “as close as ±1 second” — so your claim lands inside the opening second.

A Tatkal claim is a hold. The berth is blocked while IRCTC hands you to a bank's gateway it does not own, cannot hurry, cannot roll back. When the hold dies before the bank answers, IRCTC's advisory names the result — “money debited from the account but ticket not booked”. No undo, only compensation: IRCTC returns the amount “on next day”, the bank credits the passenger “within 2-3 working days”.

Railways widened the pipe: concurrent connections 40,000 to 300,000, capacity 2,000 to 15,000 tickets a minute. By 19 January 2016 it had gone the other way. Scripting “cannot be stopped”, so it carried a “minimum payment time check” — slowing the honest buyer to blunt the bot, widening the window his hold must survive.

From 1 July 2025 the Ministry stopped tuning the timer and rationed the online holders: Aadhaar-authenticated users only. The timer has no right value. Cut it and a buyer is debited for a berth he never gets; stretch it and the berth sits locked by whoever got there first.

Your hold expires in seconds and the gateway has not answered. Write what your system does.
