London, April 2018

A bank with one weekend to move five million people onto a platform nobody had run before.

TSB had been cut out of Lloyds Banking Group and still rented Lloyds' core banking system. Its Spanish owner, Sabadell, had told the market that moving TSB onto its own Proteo platform could deliver "approximately £160 million per annum" in IT savings. Sabadell had already migrated seven banks onto Proteo, and its method was one event: all the data, one weekend.

Over the weekend of 20–22 April, TSB moved roughly 5.2 million customers onto Proteo4UK. Reader and writer changed in the same instant. No stretch of writing both stores. Nobody spent a week serving the old answer while counting how often the new one disagreed. And by contract, no way back: migration off the Lloyds platform "was irreversible", the regulator's word.

In the first week 20–30% of retail and business customers could not pay anyone online. 26 were reading other customers' statements. Nobody went back: TSB had waived its right to the Lloyds platform, and a roll-back plan "was not possible". In the year from migration, 225,492 complaints and £32,705,762 in redress. Business as usual returned 10 December; the £48,650,000 fine came later.

The date had never really been open. TSB's own plan said so: "created on a top-down and 'right-to-left' basis" — drawn backwards from the deadline it had to hit.

Name one thing TSB could have shipped first that would have left them a way back. Write it before you read on.
