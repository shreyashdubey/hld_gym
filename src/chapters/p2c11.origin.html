London, April 2018

A bank that took the maintenance window to its maximum and still had no way back.

TSB had been divested from Lloyds Banking Group and still rented Lloyds' core banking system. Its Spanish owner, Sabadell, wanted it on Proteo4UK, by its usual method: all the data, one weekend.

So the window went to its maximum. Not four hours at 2 a.m. but the whole dress-rehearsed weekend of 20–22 April 2018, 5.2 million customers, reader and writer changing in the same instant. No stretch of writing both stores, no week counting how often they disagreed. In week one 20–30% of retail and business customers could not pay anyone online; the regulators' fine was £48,650,000.

But the way back shut before a row moved. To migrate at all, TSB had to notify Lloyds it was exiting, waiving its right to a copy of Lloyds' platform, good until 31 March 2019. TSB's board gave that up on 10 April 2018, eight days before it voted whether to go at all; after the data moved, "a very short window" remained before the Lloyds platform was disabled. The FCA's word is "irreversible"; a roll-back plan "was not possible".

Every rollback in the four phases ahead — flag off, truncate, flip the reads back, keep writing the old store for a fortnight — is a return to the old store. TSB no longer had one.

Name one thing TSB could have shipped first that would have left a way back. Write it before you read on.
