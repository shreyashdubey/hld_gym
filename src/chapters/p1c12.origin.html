ATM Forum, 1994

A committee is deciding, in silicon, how a switch says no.

A cell is 53 octets. Payload on the link runs at 149.760 Mbps. That is 353,000 verdicts a second, under three microseconds each. At that budget you do not get a log of arrival times. You get one counter and one clock read.

So UNI 3.1 writes the test twice. One version advances a Theoretical Arrival Time. The other drains a bucket counter. The spec calls them "equivalent in the sense that for any sequence of cell arrival times … the two algorithms determine the same cells to be conforming", joined by a single line: TAT = X + LCT.

Refill and drain were never two algorithms. One counter, read from opposite ends. What you actually pick is the reset rule — and a tolerance, τ, because multiplexing upstream clumps a stream that left the customer perfectly spaced. Bucket capacity is T + τ: your burst allowance is the fudge factor. Five years on, RFC 2697 rebuilt the pair for IP as "two token buckets, C and E".

τ was never solved. Set it tight and you discard cells from a customer who obeyed, then bill them. UNI 3.1 has the user pick from a set the network offers, and leaves "whether the set of values is to be standardized or to be determined by the network operator" for further study.

Your limiter has a burst knob. How would you choose its number? Write it down before you read on.
