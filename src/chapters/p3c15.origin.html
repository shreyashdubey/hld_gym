Sydney, 2009

Lars and Jens Rasmussen built Google Maps, then left it to replace email. On 28 May 2009 Lars previewed Google Wave, grown from a five-person Sydney prototype. The protocol, he wrote, was "designed for open federation".

Then read the design they published. Each wavelet — the unit the transform runs on — has "a designated wave provider that has the definitive copy", and that provider's server does the transforming, against "a single state space, which is the history of operations it has applied." Everyone else caches the wavelet and forwards their edits there.

So the federated product had one sequencer per wavelet, and editing one was only as available as that server. Google had every reason to delete it and could not: a transform is computed against an order, and an order needs someone to declare it.

Urs Hölzle announced the cancellation on 4 August 2010 — "Wave has not seen the user adoption we would have liked" — fourteen months after the preview. The code went to Apache and retired on 15 January 2018. Docs had previewed the same character-by-character editing on 15 April 2010, and nobody asked it to federate.

Play it as the candidate. The server in the middle is not a convenience you trade away. It is the part Wave could not federate.

What would you have to change about the data to delete that server? Write it first.
